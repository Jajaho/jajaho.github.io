Intel in 1976

Jakob Holz · 15 December 2024

The 8080 die is 4.8 mm on a side and carries roughly 4,500 transistors. Under a 50× objective the metal layer reads almost like a street map — you can follow the data bus from the bond pads all the way into the register file without ever losing the trace.

Preparing the die

Decapsulation is the unglamorous half. Fuming nitric acid removes the epoxy without touching the aluminium interconnect, but the margin between “clean die” and “no die” is about forty seconds. I ran three sacrificial parts before committing the C8080A to the beaker.

Every one of these traces was drawn by hand, on mylar, at 400× magnification. Somebody's whole year is sitting under this objective.

Focus stacking

The stacking itself is scripted. Each slice is aligned on a phase-correlation estimate before a Laplacian sharpness map decides which pixels survive:

# select sharpest pixel per (x, y) across the stack
def focus_stack(slices):
    sharp = [laplacian(s).var(axis=-1) for s in slices]
    idx   = np.argmax(np.stack(sharp), axis=0)
    return np.take_along_axis(np.stack(slices), idx[None], 0)[0]
